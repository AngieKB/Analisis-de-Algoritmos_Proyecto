A B S T R A C T   
The research objective of this paper is to advance knowledge about the role of artificial intelligence (AI) in complex problem-solving. A problem is complex due to the 
large number of highly inter-connected variables affecting the problem state. Complex problem-solving situations often change decremental or worsen, forcing a 
problem solver to act immediately, under considerable time pressure. While research findings support the assumption that (1) affective, (2) (meta-)cognitive, and (3) 
social processes support complex problem-solving, opportunities of AI for supporting complex problem-solving need to be further investigated. This article presents a 
scoping review of relevant literature from the last five years. The study included, N = 38 studies for coding and analysis. Our findings show that in addition to the 
increased number of publications, the current trend suggests increased quality of published work. Human-AI collaboration in complex problem-solving has been 
explored across a broad variety of AI application domains. However, the four dimensions of complex problem – namely, cognitive, metacognitive, social and affective 
- have been augmented by AI to a different extent. Although most of the work has been done in the cognitive domain, it is encouraging to see progress across social 
and affective dimensions as well. Implications for future research and practice are being discussed.   
1. Introduction 
Complex problem-solving involves engaging with problems that are 
dynamic, could have multiple solutions, and usually require multidis-
ciplinary teamwork (Dörner, 1980). These are “problems that resemble 
real-life situations” (Kunze et al., 2018, p. 3). As society has become 
more globalized and interconnected, the problems generated have 
become more complex and intractable, raising the need for skills to 
engage with new challenges and also a nuanced understanding of how to 
approach them. Fortunately, the nature of solving complex problems has 
changed over the last several decades, due to advances in computer 
science, artificial intelligence (AI), and cognitive psychology. Specif-
ically, unprecedented amounts of data, advanced algorithms, and 
significantly improved computation and distributed infrastructure (such 
as managed data lakes) now support collaboration across diverse teams. 
Working collaboratively and using technology such as an active 
agent to make sense of an abundance of information is a critical skill for 
successful learning, work, and life in general. Today’s learners and 
workers also require the ability to navigate and astutely employ a range 
of social, affective, and cognitive capabilities to make sense of and 
address complex, real-world problems (Joksimović et al., 2020, 2022; 
Microsoft, 2020). In order to successfully solve complex problems, the 
individuals or teams involved must be able to view and simulate the 
dynamic problem system in its entirety, imagining the events that would 
take place if a particular action were to be performed (de Laat et al., 
2020). 
Solving complex problems, therefore, depends on the ability of in-
dividuals to integrate diverse background knowledge and expertise into 
a dynamic collaborative process (Van Den Bossche et al., 2011). 
Research on collaborative and cooperative learning and work demon-
strates the importance of social and cognitive processes in creating a 
common understanding and shared conception of the problem and 
possible solutions (Van Den Bossche et al., 2011). Group work and 
complex problem-solving are not detached from emotions and depend 
on each individual’s motivation and abilities to plan and monitor their 
activities (Hermes & Stelling, 2016). The research in group and collab-
orative problem-solving details the importance of understanding the 
interplay of factors that influence how individuals and groups regulate 
affective and motivational processes (Hermes & Stelling, 2016).
